Parloku plans its work with OpenSpec. A spec in openspec/specs/ says how one part of Parloku behaves today: requirements, each with scenarios (WHEN this, THEN that). The puzzle

1game-rules5 req · 8 scenThe rules of Parloku as a puzzle, independent of how it is played on screen: a 6×6 mini sudoku spelled in six letters, with words threaded through it as extra clues.

Sudoku in six letters

A puzzle SHALL use exactly six distinct letters, and a solved grid SHALL have each of them once in every row, every column and every box (six boxes of two rows by three columns).

A repeated letter in a row
  • WHEN a row holds two A
  • THEN the grid is not solved, and both cells count as clashing
The boxes are two rows by three columns
  • WHEN a letter is at r1c1 and the same letter at r2c3
  • THEN they clash, because both are in the first box

Words are spelled one step at a time

Every word clue SHALL have a start cell, and a solved grid SHALL spell the word from that cell by moving at each letter to the cell above, below, left or right of the previous one. Diagonal steps SHALL NOT count.

A word down a column
  • WHEN AMICA starts at r1c1 and the grid has A M I in the first column and C A to the right of the I
  • THEN AMICA is spelled
A diagonal step
  • WHEN the only M next to a word's A is diagonal to it
  • THEN the word is not spelled from there

Given letters

A puzzle MAY fix some letters in their cells (givens). A given SHALL be part of every solution and SHALL never be moved or removed by the player.

A given in the answer
  • WHEN puzzle 2 gives I at r5c2
  • THEN its solution has I at r5c2

One solution

A puzzle that is published (in data/, or on the daily splash) SHALL have exactly one solution, and it SHALL be the answer key stored with it.

A puzzle with two solutions
  • WHEN the solver finds a second solution for a puzzle in data/
  • THEN the build reports it, and the puzzle is not published until it has one

Word sets

The words of a puzzle SHALL use exactly the puzzle's six letters between them, SHOULD be three, SHOULD be loosely linked in meaning, and SHALL NOT have the same letter twice in a row (it could never be spelled: two equal letters next to each other share a row or a column).

ITALIA PASTA ALPI
  • WHEN the words are ITALIA, PASTA and ALPI
  • THEN their letters are A I L P S T, six in all, and the set is valid
A double letter
  • WHEN a word is PIZZA
  • THEN it is refused, because the two Z would sit next to each other
2puzzle-format3 req · 4 scenHow puzzles are stored and shared: as puzzlepaw JSON files in `data/`, and as short codes that fit in a link.

Puzzle files

A puzzle SHALL be stored in data/ as puzzlepaw JSON named puzzleN-WORD1-WORD2-WORD3.json, holding the problem (letters, given letters, word clues with their start cells, 0-based) and its solution. The game SHALL read these files at build time and list them in file order.

The second puzzle
  • WHEN data/puzzle2-AMICA-GIOCO-MAGIA.json is built
  • THEN the game offers a puzzle with the words AMICA, GIOCO and MAGIA, starting at r1c1, r2c2 and r1c6

Puzzle codes

A puzzle SHALL also be written as a code LETTERS_GIVENS_WORDS, the format puzzlepaw uses: givens are <row><col><letter> and words <row><col><word>, 0-based and separated by dots. Decoding SHALL ignore case, and a code SHALL be valid only with six distinct letters and every given and word inside the grid and spelled with those letters.

The first puzzle as a code
  • WHEN puzzle 1 is encoded
  • THEN the code is AILPST_51S.23L.04P_00ITALIA.20PASTA.40ALPI
A letter that is not in the puzzle
  • WHEN a code has the given 00Z and Z is not one of its letters
  • THEN the code is not valid and the game ignores it

Links to a puzzle

Any puzzle SHALL be playable from a link with ?p=<code>, wherever p= is in the query string. A link to a puzzle the game doesn't ship SHALL add it to the puzzle choices.

A link with other options first
  • WHEN the page is opened with ?lang=it&p=AILPST_51S.23L.04P_00ITALIA.20PASTA.40ALPI
  • THEN it opens in Italian on puzzle 1

Playing

3tiles-and-dragging6 req · 9 scen · 6/6 ▶The core gesture of the game: letters are tiles that you drag from their stacks onto the board. The drag is declarative: for the tile being held, the game lists every board the drag can reach and shows the one that lands the tile closest to the finger.

Stacks of tiles

Every letter SHALL have six tiles, which sit stacked on that letter's spot below the board until placed, so the player sees one tile per letter. A letter whose six tiles are all placed SHALL show an empty spot with a ✓.

All A placed
  • WHEN six A are on the board
  • THEN the A spot below the board shows a ✓ and nothing to pull

Pulling a tile onto an empty cell

Dragging from a stack SHALL pull out one tile, and releasing it within 0.6 of a cell from the centre of an empty cell SHALL place it there. While the tile is held, the board SHALL already show where it will land.

An A onto the first cell
  • WHEN the player drags an A from its stack onto r1c1
  • THEN r1c1 holds A
Let go far from every cell
  • WHEN the player drags an A from its stack and lets go far from the board
  • THEN the tile goes back to its stack and the board doesn't change

Placed letters are never pushed out

A tile SHALL land only on an empty cell: dropping it on a letter already on the board SHALL NOT replace that letter.

Onto a given
  • WHEN the player drags an A from its stack onto the given I at r5c2
  • THEN the I stays and the A goes back to its stack

Givens stay put

A given letter SHALL NOT be moved or sent back. Dragging it, straight away or after holding it, SHALL pull a copy of its letter and leave the given where it is.

Holding a given
  • WHEN the player holds the given I at r5c2 and then drags it up one cell
  • THEN a copy of I lands on r4c2 and the given stays on r5c2

Copying and moving a placed letter

Dragging a letter on the board that is not the last letter of a word SHALL pull out a copy of it, as if from its stack, and leave the letter where it is. Holding the letter still for a moment (about a third of a second) before dragging SHALL move the letter itself.

A quick drag copies
  • WHEN C is at r1c3 and the player drags it straight away to r3c4
  • THEN there is a C at r1c3 and one at r3c4
Hold, then drag, moves
  • WHEN C is at r1c3 and the player holds it, then drags it to r3c4
  • THEN the C is at r3c4 and r1c3 is empty

Double taps

A double tap on an empty start cell SHALL fill in its word's first letter. A double tap on an empty cell that is the last gap of a row, column or box whose other five letters are all different SHALL fill in the missing letter. A double tap on a placed letter that is not a given SHALL send it back to its stack; if the letter is in a word's outline, the whole word SHALL go back (see word-snakes).

The start of MAGIA
  • WHEN r1c6 is empty and the player double taps it
  • THEN r1c6 holds M
The last gap in a row
  • WHEN a row has five different letters and one empty cell, and the player double taps that cell
  • THEN the cell gets the sixth letter
4word-snakes6 req · 9 scen · 9/9 ▶How a word in progress shows and behaves on the board: its outline (the "snake") and the gestures on its last letter (the head) that grow and move it.

The outline of a word

Every word SHALL be drawn on the board as an outline in the word's colour: a ring on its start cell while that cell is empty, growing around the letters as they are spelled from it. The outline SHALL follow the longest part of the word spelled so far from the start cell.

Two letters of MAGIA
  • WHEN r1c6 holds M and r2c6 holds A
  • THEN the MAGIA outline covers r1c6 and r2c6

The outline never steps back

A word's outline SHALL NOT use a cell twice, so a letter already in the word can't stand in for a later one.

GIOC is not GIOCO
  • WHEN GIOCO has G I O C in a row and no O after the C
  • THEN the outline covers four cells, not five
GIOCO with its own last O
  • WHEN an O is added below the C
  • THEN the outline covers all five letters

Growing a word from its head

Dragging the head of a word that is not finished (the last letter its outline covers) onto an empty cell right next to it SHALL put the word's next letter there, and the head SHALL stay where it was. The held tile SHALL show the next letter while it is over such a cell. Going on without lifting the finger to an empty cell next to the new letter SHALL add the letter after it, and so on until the word is finished.

One step down from M
  • WHEN MAGIA has only its M and the player drags the M one cell down
  • THEN that cell gets A and the M stays where it was
Several letters in one drag
  • WHEN MAGIA has only its M and the player drags the M down two cells without lifting the finger
  • THEN the word grows by A and then G, and no M is added or moved

Moving a head

Dragging the head of a word to a cell that is not next to it SHALL move that tile, never copy it.

A misplaced last letter
  • WHEN MAGIA ends with an A one cell too low and the player drags that A to the right cell
  • THEN the A moves there and its old cell is empty

Shrinking a word

Dragging the head of a word back onto the cell before it SHALL take the head's letter off the board (back to its stack), and going on backwards SHALL keep taking letters off, down to the word's first letter. Moving forward again in the same drag SHALL grow the word as usual, so a wrong turn is fixed in one gesture.

Back up two letters
  • WHEN AMICA went down the first column as A M I C A and the player drags the last A up two cells
  • THEN the A and the C go back to their stacks and AMI is left
Fix a wrong turn in one drag
  • WHEN AMICA went down as A M I C A and the player drags the last A up to the I and then right twice
  • THEN the word ends as A M I in the column, then C and A to the right of the I

Sending a snake back

A double tap on any letter of a word's outline SHALL send the whole word (every letter its outline covers that is not a given) back to the stacks. A letter outside every outline goes back alone.

Double tap inside MAGIA
  • WHEN MAGIA is spelled down the last column and the player double taps its G
  • THEN all five letters go back below and MAGIA's start ring is empty again
5player6 req · 7 scenThe game page (`index.html`): what a player can do around the board, from choosing a puzzle to winning it.

The page opens on the game

The page SHALL open straight on a playable puzzle, with the game's explanation (the notebook) below it. On a day's first visit it SHALL show the daily splash, which can be skipped (?nosplash) or shown again (?splash=first).

First visit of the day
  • WHEN a player opens the page for the first time today
  • THEN the splash greets them, and closing it shows the puzzle ready to play

Choosing a puzzle

The puzzle choice SHALL list every puzzle the game ships, labelled by its three words, plus one opened from a link. Picking a puzzle SHALL always start it afresh.

Picking puzzle 2
  • WHEN the player picks AMICA · GIOCO · MAGIA
  • THEN its empty board is shown with the three clue cards, and the link in the address bar changes to its code

Progress is kept

The board of each puzzle SHALL be remembered in the browser, so coming back to the page continues where the player left off.

Reload in the middle
  • WHEN the player has placed ten tiles and reloads the page
  • THEN the ten tiles are still there

Undo, hint, reset, link

The page SHALL offer ↶ undo (takes back the last change, any number of times), 💡 hint (glides one correct tile into an empty or wrong cell), ↺ reset (starts the puzzle again) and 🔗 link (copies a link to this puzzle).

Undo after a wrong drop
  • WHEN the player drops a tile and taps ↶
  • THEN the board is as before the drop
Hint with a wrong tile on the board
  • WHEN one tile is wrong and the player taps 💡
  • THEN a correct tile glides into a cell that was empty or wrong

Two languages

The page SHALL speak English and Italian, chosen with the EN / IT switch or ?lang=, then the last choice, then the browser's language. Every text the game shows, including the tutorial's explanations, SHALL exist in both.

An Italian browser
  • WHEN a browser set to Italian opens the page for the first time
  • THEN the page is in Italian

Winning

A solved board SHALL show a Bravo! banner with the moves and the time taken, and letter confetti.

The last tile
  • WHEN the last tile makes the grid solved
  • THEN Bravo! appears with the number of moves and the time
6logical-path3 req · 4 scenSolving like a person: the moves the 🎓 tutorial teaches, in Pietro's order, each with a reason a player can check on the board.

Pietro's method

The next logical step SHALL be found in this order: point out a wrong tile first; else put the first letter of a word whose start cell is empty; else follow a word letter by letter, where the next letter is forced when only one cell next to the previous one fits the sudoku and still lets the word be finished; else a sudoku single (a cell with one candidate, or a letter with one place in a row, column or box); else reveal a cell.

The first step of puzzle 1
  • WHEN puzzle 1 has only its givens
  • THEN the first step is "ITALIA starts in cell 1, so r1c1 is I"
A wrong tile
  • WHEN the board has a tile that differs from the solution
  • THEN the next step says that tile is not right and asks to drag it back

Every step is explained

Every step SHALL come with a sentence that names the cells and the reason (which unit already has the letter, or why a neighbour can't finish the word), in English and Italian.

A forced word letter
  • WHEN the step is a word letter with two neighbours that fit the sudoku
  • THEN the sentence says why the other neighbour can't be used

The tutorial follows the player

The tutorial SHALL ask for the next step after every drop, so it copes with any order and with mistakes, and it SHALL only use puzzles whose logical path reaches the end without reveals.

Playing ahead of the tutorial
  • WHEN the player places a correct tile the tutorial hasn't asked for yet
  • THEN the tutorial goes on from the new board

7first-principles9 req · 9 scenThe design method every Parloku screen is checked against, from the "Back to basics" devlog: ten plain steps from Norman, Nielsen, Fitts and Gestalt. A change to a screen SHALL say which of them it touches.

Write the loop

Every screen SHALL have its loop written in one sentence, and every piece on it SHALL help a step of that loop, its start or its end. For the game: look at the clues and the board, pick a letter from a stack, drop it on a cell, see if it fits; before: choose a puzzle; after: Bravo!.

A new button
  • WHEN a change adds a piece to the game screen
  • THEN it names the step of the loop the piece helps, or it doesn't go on the screen

One job per piece, sorted by when it's needed

Every piece SHALL have one job, and no two pieces the same job. Pieces needed always SHALL get the space and the middle; pieces needed once SHALL be out of the way; pieces never needed while playing SHALL leave the play area.

A stats line
  • WHEN a piece repeats what the stacks already show
  • THEN it is cut

The whole loop on one screen

On a phone (an iPhone 13 shows 664 px of page) the clues, the board and the stacks SHALL be visible together, so the player never scrolls while dragging.

The game on an iPhone 13
  • WHEN the game page is open on an iPhone 13
  • THEN the stacks end above 664 px

Things that work together sit together

Each piece SHALL sit next to what it works with: clues right above the board, stacks right below, actions under the stacks, the before-you-play choices in one row.

The language switch
  • WHEN the screen is laid out
  • THEN the language switch sits in the row with the tutorial and the puzzle choice

One signal, one meaning

A colour, shape or mark SHALL mean one thing everywhere (see theming), and important meanings SHOULD have a second cue besides colour.

A selected tile
  • WHEN something needs to look selected
  • THEN it doesn't use a letter's colour or red

Every action gets an answer

Every touch SHALL get an answer right away, and a drag SHALL show what will happen before it happens.

Phones have no hover
  • WHEN something works on mouse hover
  • THEN it also works with a tap

Mistakes are cheap

Any change to the board SHALL be undoable, and the one action that wipes the board SHALL be undoable or ask first.

Reset
  • WHEN the player taps ↺ reset by mistake
  • THEN they can get their board back

Fit fingers

Everything tapped on a touch screen SHALL be at least 44 px tall.

The action buttons on a phone
  • WHEN the page is on a touch screen
  • THEN undo, hint, reset and link are 44 px tall

Watch a real player

A change to a gesture or a screen SHALL be tried by a real player before it is called done, and their hesitations SHALL become scenarios.

A playtest finds a bug
  • WHEN a playtester can't move a word's last letter
  • THEN the case is written as a drag scenario before it is fixed
8play-screen4 req · 4 scenThe game screen itself, piece by piece, as the "Back to basics" devlog left it: what is on it, where, and what each piece tells the player.

The pieces in order

The play area SHALL show, top to bottom: the row with 🎓 tutorial, the puzzle choice and EN / IT; the clue cards; the board; the six letter stacks; the actions (undo, hint, reset, link). The rules cards and the skin switch SHALL come after the play area, and the notebook explaining the code after them.

Opening the page
  • WHEN the page is opened on a phone
  • THEN the first thing under the title is the row of choices, then the clues

Clue cards

Each word SHALL have a card in its colour above the board. Its letters SHALL colour in as they are spelled from the start cell, it SHALL show a ✓ when the word is complete, and tapping it SHALL make the word's ring or outline wiggle on the board.

Tapping a clue
  • WHEN the player taps the GIOCO card
  • THEN the GIOCO ring at r2c2 wiggles for a moment

The board

The board SHALL show 36 cells in six 2×3 boxes with thick box lines, every cell with a faint edge, the given letters with a mark that they are fixed, each word's start ring or outline, and red cells where a letter clashes (hidden while a tile is held).

A clash while dragging
  • WHEN the player holds a tile over a cell where it would clash
  • THEN the landing preview shows, without red, and the red shows after the drop

The held tile and the landing preview

A held tile SHALL grow and cast a shadow, and the board SHALL show the tile in the cell it will land in, while the other tiles slide to their places in the previewed board.

Hovering between two cells
  • WHEN the finger is between r1c2 and r1c3, nearer r1c3
  • THEN the preview shows the tile in r1c3
9theming4 req · 4 scenHow Parloku looks: CSS skins after artists and illustrators, swapped without touching the game code.

The view only names classes

The Nim code that draws the board SHALL only name classes (g0…g5 for letters, w0…w2 for words, ca/cb for cells, boxline, …) and SHALL draw every shape a skin may pick (square, rounded, round, a blob per letter). A skin SHALL be CSS only: palette, fonts and which tile shape shows.

A new skin
  • WHEN a skin is added to src/style.nim
  • THEN no file but src/style.nim changes, and the board, tiles and outlines follow it

Choosing a skin

The skin SHALL be chosen with the switch below the game or with ?skin=<name>, and remembered in the browser. Niki (after Niki de Saint Phalle) SHALL be the default for now. The ten pastel skins and the original maiolica SHALL stay available.

A link with a skin
  • WHEN the page is opened with ?skin=tullet
  • THEN it shows the Tullet skin

Colour means letter

In every skin each letter SHALL have its own colour, used for its tiles, its stack and its spelled letters on the clue cards, and nowhere else; each word SHALL have its own colour for its card, start ring and outline. Red SHALL mean only "this breaks a rule".

A clash in the Niki skin
  • WHEN two A share a row
  • THEN both cells turn red, and no letter or word uses that red

Readable letters

A skin SHALL keep the letter on every tile readable against the tile's colour and the six tile colours apart from each other; the skins devlog computes contrast and colour distance at build time for this.

A pale tile
  • WHEN a skin gives a tile a colour too close to its letter's ink
  • THEN the skins devlog build flags it

Making

10solver3 req · 3 scenCounting solutions: the backtracking solver that checks puzzles at build time and powers 💡, and its larger sibling in puzzlepaw (`parloku/solver`) that the builder uses.

Build-time check

Building the game SHALL solve every puzzle in data/, report whether it has one solution, and say whether that solution matches the stored answer key.

The shipped puzzles
  • WHEN the game is built
  • THEN each puzzle prints "1 solution (unique), matches the answer key in data/" and the path of each word

Stop early

The solver SHALL stop after a given number of solutions (2 in the game, enough to tell unique from not; 10 in the builder, shown as 10+), and it SHALL prune with the word clues, where an empty cell can stand for any letter.

Two solutions found
  • WHEN a setup has many solutions
  • THEN the game's solver returns after the second one

One rule for words

The game's solver, the builder's solver and the outlines on the board SHALL agree on how a word may run through the grid (see the settle-word-overlaps change: today they don't).

A word that comes back on itself
  • WHEN a setup only works if a word steps back onto one of its own cells
  • THEN every part of Parloku gives the same answer about it
11builder5 req · 5 scenTBD - created by archiving change add-builder. Update Purpose after archive.

Building by playing

The builder SHALL use the game's board, tiles and drag gestures. Each word SHALL have a small ring at the top that is dragged onto a cell to start the word there, and the word SHALL grow from its head as in the game.

Starting a word
  • WHEN the author drags MAGIA's ring onto r1c6
  • THEN r1c6 gets M and MAGIA starts there

Pencil marks

Every empty cell SHALL show, in pencil, the letters it can still have in at least one complete grid that fits the letters placed and all the words; a cell with none SHALL turn red. A double tap on a cell with one pencil mark SHALL fill it in.

No room left
  • WHEN the placed letters leave a cell with no possible letter
  • THEN that cell is red

Counting solutions

The builder SHALL count the complete grids that keep every placed letter and contain all the words, trying every start for the words not yet placed, and show 0, the exact number up to 9, or 10+.

Ready
  • WHEN the count is 1
  • THEN the puzzle made of the word starts and the pinned letters is ready, and 🔗 gives its play link

Givens are pinned

Letters placed in the builder SHALL be only for exploring; the author SHALL pin a letter (📌) to make it a given, and the puzzle SHALL be the word starts plus the pinned letters.

Pin a letter
  • WHEN the author drags the pin onto a placed letter
  • THEN the letter becomes a given in the play link

Save and share

💾 SHALL copy a link that reopens the builder exactly as it is (?s=), and 🔗 SHALL copy the link to play the puzzle (?p=).

Reopening a draft
  • WHEN the author opens a 💾 link
  • THEN the builder shows the same letters, words, pins and letter order

Working

12way-of-working5 req · 5 scenHow Parloku is made: pages that explain themselves, a devlog and a digital garden, previews before merges, and OpenSpec to keep every agreed behaviour written down and checked.

Pages are explainers

Every page (the game, the builder, each post) SHALL be a nimib notebook whose source is the page: the code that runs is the code shown, run natively at build time for the printed outputs and compiled to JavaScript for the browser. A page SHALL be a complete standalone HTML file served by GitHub Pages, with nothing loaded from claude.ai.

A new page
  • WHEN a page is added
  • THEN it has a src/<name>.nim notebook that writes it, and a build line in the README

Devlog and garden

Work SHALL be written up in two places. The devlog (devlog/<slug>/) SHALL hold dated posts about what changed in the game and why, listed newest first in the README. The digital garden (garden/<slug>/) SHALL hold living notes and prototypes of ideas that are still growing, undated, that may never reach the game; an idea leaves the garden for the game through an OpenSpec change.

A prototype that works out
  • WHEN a garden prototype (like the dividing tiles) is to become part of the game
  • THEN an OpenSpec change proposes it, names the garden page, and the devlog post comes when it ships

Preview first, one clean PR

A change SHALL be shown to Pietro as a published preview (an artifact, which opens on a phone) before anything is merged. Nothing SHALL be pushed to main until Pietro says so, and each change SHALL arrive as one clean PR. Old branches SHALL be kept.

A gesture fix
  • WHEN a gesture is fixed
  • THEN Pietro gets a preview link to try it on his phone, and the PR waits for his go

Behaviour lives in the specs

Every agreed behaviour SHALL be written in openspec/specs/ as a requirement with scenarios. New work SHALL start as an OpenSpec change (openspec/changes/<name>/: proposal, spec deltas, tasks), and when it ships the change SHALL be archived so its deltas merge into the specs. A playtest finding SHALL become a scenario before it is fixed.

A playtest bug
  • WHEN a playtester finds that moving a word's last letter copies it
  • THEN the case is written as a drag scenario in a change, it fails, the fix makes it pass, and archiving moves it into the specs

Scenarios that run

A gameplay scenario SHALL be written as a ```drag block where it can be (a start board, a gesture, an end board or an outline), and nim r tools/specdrag.nim SHALL play every one of them on the built game page (headless Chromium, real pointer events). Scenarios in the specs SHALL pass before a PR is merged; scenarios in an open change MAY fail until it is built. The spec viewer (specs/) SHALL play each one as an animation with its result.

A spec scenario breaks
  • WHEN a change to src/parloku.nim makes a scenario in openspec/specs/ fail
  • THEN tools/specdrag.nim exits with an error naming the scenario, and shows the expected and the actual board

Proposal

Why

Pietro reviewed the player's menu (5 November 2026): there are too many buttons at the top, and a reset can't be taken back. Built in PR #13 (project thread "Evolve Parloku game and devlog"), which is waiting for his review.

Proposal

Why

Writing the specs showed three parts of Parloku disagreeing on one rule: may a word pass twice over the same cell?

  • The board's outlines never use a cell twice (since PR #8: G-I-O-C no longer reads as GIOCO).
  • The game's solver (trace(..., revisit = true)) lets a word step back onto its own cells.
  • The builder's solver (PR #6) forbids it, also forbids two words on one cell "for now", and has an optional "no U-turn" filter.

A puzzle could then be unique for the builder and ambiguous for the game, or be solved in the game while its outline never completes. This is a rule of the puzzle, so it is Pietro's call.

What Changes

  • Decision needed: (a) a word never uses the same cell twice, (b) whether two different words may share a cell, (c) whether U-turns are allowed.
  • Once decided: one trace rule in the game, the builder and puzzlepaw's solver, and the rule in game-rules.
