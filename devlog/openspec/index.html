Specs that play

The playtest of puzzle 2 found four things wrong with the word snakes. Before fixing more, we write down how Parloku should behave, and make the gameplay parts of it run.

Parloku's rules and gestures were spread across PR descriptions, devlog posts, code comments and chat. Each fix was right on its own, and nothing stopped the next fix from undoing it. Pietro asked for one place where the behaviour is written down, kept up to date and checked. That place is OpenSpec, set up the way nanuq uses it, and adapted to a game.

This post is the adoption, for Pietro to check. It shows what was set up, how a gameplay scenario becomes a small animation that is also a test, what writing the specs turned up, and at the end every spec and every open change.

01 · THE IDEASpecs say what is, changes say what's next

OpenSpec keeps two kinds of files in openspec/:

  • A spec (openspec/specs/<capability>/spec.md) says how one part of Parloku behaves today. It is a list of requirements ("dragging a head SHALL move it, never copy it"), each with scenarios: WHEN this happens, THEN that.
  • A change (openspec/changes/<name>/) proposes new behaviour: a proposal (why and what), spec deltas (ADDED, MODIFIED or REMOVED requirements), and tasks. When the change ships it is archived, and its deltas are merged into the specs.

So the specs are always the truth about the game, and the open changes are the plan. Work goes propose → build → archive, with the /opsx:propose, /opsx:apply and /opsx:archive commands that openspec init installed for Claude in .claude/.

03 · SCENARIOS THAT PLAYA board, a gesture, a board

Most scenarios are sentences. The gameplay ones can be more: a start board, a gesture and what the board must look like after it. They are written in a drag block right under the scenario, like this one from word-snakes:

#### Scenario: A misplaced last letter
- **WHEN** MAGIA ends with an A one cell too low and the player drags that A to the right cell
- **THEN** the A moves there and its old cell is empty

```drag
puzzle: 2
start:
  . . . . . M
  . . . . . A
  . . . . . G
  . . . . . I
  . I O . . A
  . . . . I O
drag: r5c6 -> r4c5
end:
  . . . . . M
  . . . . . A
  . . . . . G
  . . . . A I
  . I O . . .
  . . . . I O
outline MAGIA: r1c6 r2c6 r3c6 r4c6 r4c5
```

drag: pulls straight away, hold: holds first (to move a letter), and a waypoint is a cell, below A (the letter's tile under the board) or off. outline checks the cells a word's outline covers, and grab: refused says nothing may be picked up.

nim r tools/specdrag.nim plays every scenario on the built game page, in headless Chromium on an iPhone 13, the way tests/gestures/ already did for the snake fixes. It hands the start board to the page as saved progress, does the gesture with real pointer events, and reads the end board and each word's outline back from what the page draws. So it checks what a player gets, whichever part of the code does it (the declarative drag spec, or the snake gestures that are written by hand in the browser wiring). Nothing in the game had to change for it. Every frame is recorded, and the viewer replays it with the page's own drawing of tiles and outlines: the animation is not an illustration, it is what the game did.

06 · HOW WE WORK FROM NOWFive habits

  1. A playtest finding becomes a scenario first. It fails, then the fix makes it pass. That is how the four snake cases went in.
  2. New work starts as a change. /opsx:propose writes the proposal, the spec deltas and the tasks; the PR builds it; /opsx:archive merges the deltas into the specs and adds the devlog line.
  3. Previews first, one clean PR. Unchanged: you see an artifact on your phone before anything merges, nothing goes to main without your go, and old branches stay.
  4. Devlog and garden. The devlog is dated posts, each about one step in how the game evolves. The digital garden (after Maggie Appleton) is articles that stand apart from the posts: published before they are complete, then come back to and grown. How the garden works is still taking shape, and the way-of-working spec says so.
  5. Specs in English, short and plain, like the devlog. Each says SHALL for what must hold and SHOULD for what is preferred.

The puzzle

1game-rules5 req · 8 scenThe rules of Parloku as a puzzle, independent of how it is played on screen: a 6×6 mini sudoku spelled in six letters, with words threaded through it as extra clues.

Sudoku in six letters

A puzzle SHALL use exactly six distinct letters, and a solved grid SHALL have each of them once in every row, every column and every box (six boxes of two rows by three columns).

A repeated letter in a row
  • WHEN a row holds two A
  • THEN the grid is not solved, and both cells count as clashing
The boxes are two rows by three columns
  • WHEN a letter is at r1c1 and the same letter at r2c3
  • THEN they clash, because both are in the first box

Words are spelled one step at a time

Every word clue SHALL have a start cell, and a solved grid SHALL spell the word from that cell by moving at each letter to the cell above, below, left or right of the previous one. Diagonal steps SHALL NOT count.

A word down a column
  • WHEN AMICA starts at r1c1 and the grid has A M I in the first column and C A to the right of the I
  • THEN AMICA is spelled
A diagonal step
  • WHEN the only M next to a word's A is diagonal to it
  • THEN the word is not spelled from there

Given letters

A puzzle MAY fix some letters in their cells (givens). A given SHALL be part of every solution and SHALL never be moved or removed by the player.

A given in the answer
  • WHEN puzzle 2 gives I at r5c2
  • THEN its solution has I at r5c2

One solution

A puzzle that is published (in data/) SHALL have exactly one solution, and it SHALL be the answer key stored with it.

A puzzle with two solutions
  • WHEN the solver finds a second solution for a puzzle in data/
  • THEN the build reports it, and the puzzle is not published until it has one

Word sets

The words of a puzzle SHALL use exactly the puzzle's six letters between them, SHOULD be three, SHOULD be loosely linked in meaning, and SHALL NOT have the same letter twice in a row (it could never be spelled: two equal letters next to each other share a row or a column).

ITALIA PASTA ALPI
  • WHEN the words are ITALIA, PASTA and ALPI
  • THEN their letters are A I L P S T, six in all, and the set is valid
A double letter
  • WHEN a word is PIZZA
  • THEN it is refused, because the two Z would sit next to each other
2puzzle-format3 req · 4 scenHow puzzles are stored and shared: as puzzlepaw JSON files in `data/`, and as short codes that fit in a link.

Puzzle files

A puzzle SHALL be stored in data/ as puzzlepaw JSON named puzzleN-WORD1-WORD2-WORD3.json, holding the problem (letters, given letters, word clues with their start cells, 0-based) and its solution. The game SHALL read these files at build time and list them in file order.

The second puzzle
  • WHEN data/puzzle2-AMICA-GIOCO-MAGIA.json is built
  • THEN the game offers a puzzle with the words AMICA, GIOCO and MAGIA, starting at r1c1, r2c2 and r1c6

Puzzle codes

A puzzle SHALL also be written as a code LETTERS_GIVENS_WORDS, the format puzzlepaw uses: givens are <row><col><letter> and words <row><col><word>, 0-based and separated by dots. Decoding SHALL ignore case, and a code SHALL be valid only with six distinct letters and every given and word inside the grid and spelled with those letters.

The first puzzle as a code
  • WHEN puzzle 1 is encoded
  • THEN the code is AILPST_51S.23L.04P_00ITALIA.20PASTA.40ALPI
A letter that is not in the puzzle
  • WHEN a code has the given 00Z and Z is not one of its letters
  • THEN the code is not valid and the game ignores it

Links to a puzzle

Any puzzle SHALL be playable from a link with ?p=<code>, wherever p= is in the query string. A link to a puzzle the game doesn't ship SHALL add it to the puzzle choices.

A link with other options first
  • WHEN the page is opened with ?lang=it&p=AILPST_51S.23L.04P_00ITALIA.20PASTA.40ALPI
  • THEN it opens in Italian on puzzle 1

Looking

3first-principles9 req · 9 scenThe design method every Parloku screen is checked against, from the "Back to basics" devlog: ten plain steps from Norman, Nielsen, Fitts and Gestalt. A change to a screen SHALL say which of them it touches.

Write the loop

Every screen SHALL have its loop written in one sentence, and every piece on it SHALL help a step of that loop, its start or its end. For the game: look at the clues and the board, pull a copy of a letter from below, drop it on a cell, see if it fits; before: choose a puzzle; after: Bravo!.

A new button
  • WHEN a change adds a piece to the game screen
  • THEN it names the step of the loop the piece helps, or it doesn't go on the screen

One job per piece, sorted by when it's needed

Every piece SHALL have one job, and no two pieces the same job. Pieces needed always SHALL get the space and the middle; pieces needed once SHALL be out of the way; pieces never needed while playing SHALL leave the play area.

A stats line
  • WHEN a piece repeats what the letters below already show
  • THEN it is cut

The whole loop on one screen

On a phone (an iPhone 13 shows 664 px of page) the clues, the board and the six letters SHALL be visible together, so the player never scrolls while dragging.

The game on an iPhone 13
  • WHEN the game page is open on an iPhone 13
  • THEN the six letters end above 664 px

Things that work together sit together

Each piece SHALL sit next to what it works with: clues right above the board, the letters right below it, and everything that is not the loop (istruzioni, the puzzle choice, restart) together in the one menu at the top.

The menu
  • WHEN the player opens the menu
  • THEN istruzioni, ancora! and rifaccio are in one bar at the top, away from the board

One signal, one meaning

A colour, shape or mark SHALL mean one thing everywhere (see theming), and important meanings SHOULD have a second cue besides colour.

A selected tile
  • WHEN something needs to look selected
  • THEN it doesn't use a letter's colour or red

Every action gets an answer

Every touch SHALL get an answer right away, and a drag SHALL show what will happen before it happens.

Phones have no hover
  • WHEN something works on mouse hover
  • THEN it also works with a tap

Mistakes are cheap

Any single move SHALL be easy to take back with the same gestures that made it (a double tap or a drag sends a letter back), and the one action that wipes the board SHALL be undoable right after it, without a confirmation in the way.

Reset
  • WHEN the player taps reset by mistake
  • THEN they can get their board back with one tap

Fit fingers

Everything tapped on a touch screen SHALL be at least 44 px tall.

The action buttons on a phone
  • WHEN the page is on a touch screen
  • THEN the menu buttons (istruzioni, ancora!, rifaccio) are 44 px tall

Watch a real player

A change to a gesture or a screen SHALL be tried by a real player before it is called done, and their hesitations SHALL become scenarios.

A playtest finds a bug
  • WHEN a playtester can't move a word's last letter
  • THEN the case is written as a drag scenario before it is fixed
4play-screen4 req · 6 scenThe game screen itself, piece by piece: what is on it, where, and what each piece tells the player.

The pieces in order

The game page SHALL show only the clue cards, the board and the six letters below it, top to bottom, on one screen with nothing to scroll to; the menu (istruzioni, ancora!, rifaccio) SHALL slide down from the top on a tap above the grid. The rules are told on the istruzioni page. The rules cards, the language switch and the notebook explaining the code are still in the page's HTML but SHALL NOT show while playing.

Opening the page
  • WHEN the page is opened on a phone
  • THEN the clues, the board and the six letters fill the screen, with no buttons above them and nothing below

Clue cards

Each word SHALL have a card in its colour above the board. Its letters SHALL colour in as they are spelled from the start cell, it SHALL show a ✓ when the word is complete, and tapping it SHALL make the word's ring or outline wiggle on the board.

Tapping a clue
  • WHEN the player taps the GIOCO card
  • THEN the GIOCO ring at r2c2 wiggles for a moment

The board

The board SHALL be the classic mini sudoku grid: 36 cells in six 2×3 boxes, with thin lines between cells and thick lines between boxes (?grid=rounded keeps the earlier rounded cells for now). It SHALL show the given letters with a mark that they are fixed, each word's start mark or outline, and letters in conflict rolling like marbles (more over a few seconds) instead of red cells.

A clash
  • WHEN two A share a row
  • THEN both A roll from side to side, and no cell turns red
The old grid
  • WHEN the page is opened with ?grid=rounded
  • THEN the board shows the earlier rounded cells
A clash while dragging
  • WHEN the player holds a tile over a cell where it would clash
  • THEN the landing preview shows the tile in that cell, and the letters roll once it is dropped there

The held tile and the landing preview

A held tile SHALL grow and cast a shadow, and the board SHALL show the tile in the cell it will land in, while the other tiles slide to their places in the previewed board.

Hovering between two cells
  • WHEN the finger is between r1c2 and r1c3, nearer r1c3
  • THEN the preview shows the tile in r1c3
5theming4 req · 4 scenHow Parloku looks: the Niki skin (after Niki de Saint Phalle), as CSS that can be swapped without touching the game code.

The view only names classes

The Nim code that draws the board SHALL only name classes (g0…g5 for letters, w0…w2 for words, ca/cb for cells, boxline, …) and SHALL draw every shape a skin may pick (square, rounded, round, a blob per letter). A skin SHALL be CSS only: palette, fonts and which tile shape shows.

A new skin
  • WHEN a skin is added to src/style.nim
  • THEN no file but src/style.nim changes, and the board, tiles and outlines follow it

Choosing a skin

The skin SHALL be chosen with the switch below the game or with ?skin=<name>, and remembered in the browser. Niki (after Niki de Saint Phalle) SHALL be the default for now. The ten pastel skins and the original maiolica SHALL stay available.

A link with a skin
  • WHEN the page is opened with ?skin=tullet
  • THEN it shows the Tullet skin

Colour means letter

In every skin each letter SHALL have its own colour, used for its tiles and its spelled letters on the clue cards, and nowhere else; each word SHALL have its own colour for its card, start mark and outline. A broken rule SHALL be shown by motion (the clashing letters roll), not by a colour.

A clash in the Niki skin
  • WHEN two A share a row
  • THEN both roll, and no colour on the board changes

Readable letters

A skin SHALL keep the letter on every tile readable against the tile's colour and the six tile colours apart from each other; the skins devlog computes contrast and colour distance at build time for this.

A pale tile
  • WHEN a skin gives a tile a colour too close to its letter's ink
  • THEN the skins devlog build flags it

Playing

6tiles-and-dragging6 req · 9 scen · 6/6 ▶The core gesture of the game: each letter is one tile below the board, and you pull copies off it onto the board. The drag is declarative: for the tile being held, the game lists every board the drag can reach and shows the one that lands the tile closest to the finger.

Tiles that divide

Below the board each of the six letters SHALL show as one tile (in two rows of three). Pulling it SHALL stretch a neck from the letter until the copy splits off, and the letter left behind SHALL ripple; a copy brought back to its letter before letting go SHALL reach out and merge into it. A letter has six copies: once all six are on the board, its spot SHALL show an empty ring with a ✓, and nothing can be pulled from it.

All A placed
  • WHEN six A are on the board
  • THEN the A spot below the board shows an empty ring with a ✓ and nothing to pull

Pulling a tile onto an empty cell

Dragging a letter from below SHALL pull out one copy, and releasing it within 0.6 of a cell from the centre of an empty cell SHALL place it there. While the tile is held, the board SHALL already show where it will land.

An A onto the first cell
  • WHEN the player pulls an A from below onto r1c1
  • THEN r1c1 holds A
Let go far from every cell
  • WHEN the player pulls an A from below and lets go far from the board
  • THEN the copy goes back and merges into its letter, and the board doesn't change

Placed letters are never pushed out

A tile SHALL land only on an empty cell: dropping it on a letter already on the board SHALL NOT replace that letter.

Onto a given
  • WHEN the player pulls an A from below onto the given I at r5c2
  • THEN the I stays and the A goes back below

Givens stay put

A given letter SHALL NOT be moved or sent back. Dragging it, straight away or after holding it, SHALL pull a copy of its letter and leave the given where it is.

Holding a given
  • WHEN the player holds the given I at r5c2 and then drags it up one cell
  • THEN a copy of I lands on r4c2 and the given stays on r5c2

Copying and moving a placed letter

Dragging a letter on the board that is not the last letter of a word SHALL pull out a copy of it, as if from its letter below, and leave the letter where it is. Holding the letter still for a moment (about a third of a second) before dragging SHALL move the letter itself; let go away from every empty cell, it SHALL stay in its cell.

A quick drag copies
  • WHEN C is at r1c3 and the player drags it straight away to r3c4
  • THEN there is a C at r1c3 and one at r3c4
Hold, then drag, moves
  • WHEN C is at r1c3 and the player holds it, then drags it to r3c4
  • THEN the C is at r3c4 and r1c3 is empty

Double taps

A double tap on an empty start cell SHALL fill in its word's first letter. A double tap on an empty cell that is the last gap of a row, column or box whose other five letters are all different SHALL fill in the missing letter. A double tap on a placed letter that is not a given SHALL send it back below, into its letter; if the letter is in a word's outline, the whole word SHALL go back (see word-snakes).

The start of MAGIA
  • WHEN r1c6 is empty and the player double taps it
  • THEN r1c6 holds M
The last gap in a row
  • WHEN a row has five different letters and one empty cell, and the player double taps that cell
  • THEN the cell gets the sixth letter
7word-snakes6 req · 9 scen · 9/9 ▶How a word in progress shows and behaves on the board: its outline (the "snake") and the gestures on its last letter (the head) that grow and move it.

The outline of a word

Every word SHALL be drawn on the board as an outline in the word's colour: a thin rounded square on its start cell while the word has at most one letter, growing around the letters as they are spelled from it. The outline SHALL stay inside its cells, so two words in cells side by side never touch, and SHALL follow the longest part of the word spelled so far from the start cell.

Two letters of MAGIA
  • WHEN r1c6 holds M and r2c6 holds A
  • THEN the MAGIA outline covers r1c6 and r2c6

The outline never steps back

A word's outline SHALL NOT use a cell twice, so a letter already in the word can't stand in for a later one.

GIOC is not GIOCO
  • WHEN GIOCO has G I O C in a row and no O after the C
  • THEN the outline covers four cells, not five
GIOCO with its own last O
  • WHEN an O is added below the C
  • THEN the outline covers all five letters

Growing a word from its head

Dragging the head of a word that is not finished (the last letter its outline covers) onto an empty cell right next to it SHALL put the word's next letter there, and the head SHALL stay where it was. The held tile SHALL show the next letter while it is over such a cell. Going on without lifting the finger to an empty cell next to the new letter SHALL add the letter after it, and so on until the word is finished.

One step down from M
  • WHEN MAGIA has only its M and the player drags the M one cell down
  • THEN that cell gets A and the M stays where it was
Several letters in one drag
  • WHEN MAGIA has only its M and the player drags the M down two cells without lifting the finger
  • THEN the word grows by A and then G, and no M is added or moved

Moving a head

Dragging the head of a word to a cell that is not next to it SHALL move that tile, never copy it.

A misplaced last letter
  • WHEN MAGIA ends with an A one cell too low and the player drags that A to the right cell
  • THEN the A moves there and its old cell is empty

Shrinking a word

Dragging the head of a word back onto the cell before it SHALL take the head's letter off the board (back below), and going on backwards SHALL keep taking letters off, down to the word's first letter. Moving forward again in the same drag SHALL grow the word as usual, so a wrong turn is fixed in one gesture.

Back up two letters
  • WHEN AMICA went down the first column as A M I C A and the player drags the last A up two cells
  • THEN the A and the C go back below and AMI is left
Fix a wrong turn in one drag
  • WHEN AMICA went down as A M I C A and the player drags the last A up to the I and then right twice
  • THEN the word ends as A M I in the column, then C and A to the right of the I

Sending a snake back

A double tap on any letter of a word's outline SHALL send the whole word (every letter its outline covers that is not a given) back below. A letter outside every outline goes back alone.

Double tap inside MAGIA
  • WHEN MAGIA is spelled down the last column and the player double taps its G
  • THEN all five letters go back below and MAGIA's start ring is empty again
8player7 req · 11 scenThe game page (`index.html`): what a player can do around the board, from choosing a puzzle to winning it.

The page opens on the game

The page SHALL open straight on a playable puzzle, and show only the game (see play-screen). On a day's first visit it SHALL show the daily splash, which can be skipped (?nosplash) or shown again (?splash=first).

nbRawHtml: """<h2><span class="num">01 · THE IDEA</span>Specs say what is, changes say what's next</h2>"""

nbText: """
OpenSpec keeps two kinds of files in `openspec/`:

- A **spec** (`openspec/specs/<capability>/spec.md`) says how one part of Parloku behaves *today*. It is a list of
  requirements ("dragging a head SHALL move it, never copy it"), each with scenarios: WHEN this happens, THEN that.
- A **change** (`openspec/changes/<name>/`) proposes new behaviour: a proposal (why and what), spec deltas (ADDED,
  MODIFIED or REMOVED requirements), and tasks. When the change ships it is *archived*, and its deltas are merged into
  the specs.

So the specs are always the truth about the game, and the open changes are the plan. Work goes
propose → build → archive, with the `/opsx:propose`, `/opsx:apply` and `/opsx:archive` commands that
`openspec init` installed for Claude in `.claude/`.
"""
