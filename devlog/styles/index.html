Twenty styles for Parloku

Twenty artists and designers, none of them from the first round of skins. Each gets a play area in the new layout, then a note on who they are and what I borrowed, with links and a small gallery. Just scroll.

Every play area shows the same moment of puzzle 1 (ITALIA · PASTA · ALPI). ITALIA is spelled, PASTA is half done, and ALPI has not started, so its start cell has only the ring. Each area has the parts of the new layout and nothing else:

  • Three clue words, one per line, aligned left, centre and right, each in a dark frame. No numbers: the frame's colour is the word's colour on the board.
  • The board, with the outline of each spelled word drawn in its colour, and a ring on the start cell of a word that has no letters yet. A dotted ring marks the three given letters, as in the game.
  • Six budding letters below the board, one per symbol. The I is being pulled, so a copy buds off it on a neck, as in the cell-division prototype.

Each style picks six pastel colours for the letters, three dark colours for the words, a tile shape and one letter face. The small grey line under each board shows the checks the page runs while it builds: letter contrast on the tiles, the smallest colour difference (ΔE) between two tiles, and how far apart the three word colours are.

How I picked them. I started over, with three rules. Each person had to have made things for children, or made things children love. Their look had to survive being reduced to a palette, a shape and a typeface. And the list had to travel: Italy, France and Japan as Pietro asked, then Germany, Sweden, the US, Canada, Mexico, Brazil, South Africa and India. Eight of the twenty are women. I borrowed only principles (palettes, shapes, edges, type), never characters or motifs.

About the galleries. Pictures come from Wikimedia Commons, where every file is freely licensed, and they are linked from there, not copied here. The credit under each one names the author and licence. Commons has no free picture for many of these works, since most are still under copyright, and claude.ai pages block remote images altogether. Wherever a picture is missing, the card shows a small abstract sketch in the style's own palette instead. Tap any card to open an image search for the real work, or "about" for its page.

designer of objects, furniture and games · 1932–2020

Who

Enzo Mari believed a designed object should teach you something while you use it. He drew chairs anyone could build with a saw and nails, calendars, vases, and many games for children. His most famous puzzle, 16 animali (1957, for Danese), is a single slab of wood cut into sixteen animals that lock together with no gaps. With Iela Mari he also made wordless picture books such as La mela e la farfalla.

What I took. A puzzle whose pieces fit with nothing left over is exactly what a wordoku is. The tiles are square and cut close, with a thin black outline like a jigsaw cut, on a birch-plywood ground. The colours are his toy primaries, softened.

Why for Parloku. Grown-ups know his work from design museums, and children just see animals and games.

Who

Yayoi Kusama has covered pumpkins, rooms, bodies and whole trees in polka dots for seventy years. In The Obliteration Room (first shown in 2002) visitors, most of them children, walk into an all-white apartment and are handed sheets of coloured dot stickers. A few weeks later the room has vanished under dots.

What I took. The Obliteration Room is a white board that gets filled with coloured dots, which is a game of Parloku. So the board is pure white, every tile is a perfect round sticker, and a few faint dots float in the background.

Why for Parloku. It is the most child-led artwork I know. The finished room is made by children.

Who

Raoul Dufy painted regattas, racecourses, orchestras and the seafront at Nice in a way that looks fast and happy. He noticed that the eye catches colour before outline, so he let them separate: a patch of colour, then a quick ink drawing that only half sits on it. He also designed fabrics for Bianchini-Férier and painted La Fée Électricité (1937), one of the largest paintings in the world, now in the Musée d'Art Moderne de Paris.

What I took. Colour and line slip apart. Each tile is a soft round patch of colour, and the letter's ink ring sits a little off it, as if drawn in one quick stroke. The letters use a handwriting face, and everything is navy ink on a seaside white.

Why for Parloku. It is how children colour in, with the colour going over the lines, and Dufy made it elegant.

Who

Josef Albers taught at the Bauhaus and then in America, and spent 26 years painting one idea: Homage to the Square, three or four squares nested inside each other, a little lower than centre. He wanted to show that a colour changes depending on its neighbours. His book Interaction of Color (1963) is still how designers learn colour.

What I took. Every tile is a small Homage: a pastel square with a lighter square set low inside it, and the letter on the inner square. The six colours were picked as one family, the way he would arrange them, with warm and cool alternating.

Why for Parloku. It is the most careful study of colour there is, and it fits a game where colour is the main information.

Who

Giorgio Morandi hardly left Bologna and painted almost the same thing for forty years: a few bottles, jugs and boxes on a table, in his studio on Via Fondazza. The colours are dusty: bone, putty, faded rose, grey-blue. He even painted the bottles themselves so they would hold the light the way he wanted. The Museo Morandi in Bologna has his studio.

What I took. Calm. Six powdery colours that are still easy to tell apart, soft rounded tiles with no outline, a warm grey ground, and a serif for the letters. It is the one style here that asks you to slow down.

Why for Parloku. A pastel palette for grown-ups. If the others are too loud, this is the answer.

Who

Mitsumasa Anno was a school teacher before he made picture books, and you can tell. Anno's Journey (1977) has no words, just a rider crossing a watercolour Europe full of hidden jokes. Anno's Counting Book, Anno's Magic Seeds and Anno's Mysterious Multiplying Jar teach counting, doubling and factorials as stories. He won the Hans Christian Andersen Award in 1984, and there is a museum for him in his home town of Tsuwano.

What I took. Watercolour, and the joy of logic. The tiles are round washes with soft edges, the colours are thin and transparent, and the ground is paper. The rounded type is the kind used in Japanese children's books.

Why for Parloku. No one made maths friendlier for children, and Parloku is a logic game.

Who

Roberto Burle Marx painted with plants. He designed gardens as if they were abstract paintings, with sweeping beds of a single species, and he collected and named dozens of Brazilian plants. What I took. Curves and gardens. Tiles are smooth pebbles, the ground carries faint wave bands like his pavements, the colours are leaf, flower and sea, and the letters are a round geometric face.

Why for Parloku. Our new tiles bud and divide like living cells, so a style about gardens fits them.

Why for Parloku. He is the Italian artist who took toys and advertising seriously, and both are part of this game.

Who

Hilma af Klint painted abstract pictures years before Kandinsky and Mondrian, and asked that they stay hidden until twenty years after her death. The Ten Largest (1907) are three-metre canvases about childhood, youth, adulthood and old age, full of pastel spirals, petals and letters. type Rgb = tuple[r, g, b: float]
proc hex(s: string): Rgb =
  let h = s.strip(chars = {'#', ' '})
  (parseHexInt(h[0..1]) / 255, parseHexInt(h[2..3]) / 255, parseHexInt(h[4..5]) / 255)
proc toHex(c: Rgb): string =
  "#" & [c.r, c.g, c.b].mapIt(toHex(int(round(clamp(it, 0.0, 1.0) * 255)), 2).toLowerAscii).join
proc mix(a, b: string, t: float): string =
  let (x, y) = (hex(a), hex(b))
  toHex((x.r + (y.r - x.r) * t, x.g + (y.g - x.g) * t, x.b + (y.b - x.b) * t))
proc lin(u: float): float = (if u <= 0.04045: u / 12.92 else: pow((u + 0.055) / 1.055, 2.4))
proc luminance(c: Rgb): float = 0.2126 * lin(c.r) + 0.7152 * lin(c.g) + 0.0722 * lin(c.b)
proc contrast(a, b: string): float =
  let (x, y) = (luminance(hex(a)), luminance(hex(b)))
  (max(x, y) + 0.05) / (min(x, y) + 0.05)
proc lab(s: string): (float, float, float) =
  let c = hex(s)
  let (r, g, b) = (lin(c.r), lin(c.g), lin(c.b))
  let x = (r * 0.4124 + g * 0.3576 + b * 0.1805) / 0.95047
  let y = r * 0.2126 + g * 0.7152 + b * 0.0722
  let z = (r * 0.0193 + g * 0.1192 + b * 0.9505) / 1.08883
  proc f(v: float): float = (if v > 0.008856: cbrt(v) else: 7.787 * v + 16 / 116)
  (116 * f(y) - 16, 500 * (f(x) - f(y)), 200 * (f(y) - f(z)))
proc deltaE(a, b: string): float =
  let (p, q) = (lab(a), lab(b))
  sqrt((p[0] - q[0])^2 + (p[1] - q[1])^2 + (p[2] - q[2])^2)

func f1(x: float): string = formatFloat(x, ffDecimal, 1)

proc blobPath(cx, cy, s: float, seed: int, amp: float): string =
  ## a smooth closed blob around (cx, cy): 8 points with varied radii, joined by Catmull-Rom curves
  var pts: seq[(float, float)]
  for k in 0 ..< 8:
    let a = k.float * PI / 4 + 0.3 * sin(seed.float)
    let rr = s * (1 + amp * sin(seed.float * 1.7 + k.float * 2.3) * (if k mod 2 == 0: 1.0 else: -0.6))
    pts.add (cx + rr * cos(a), cy + rr * sin(a))
  result = &"M{f1(pts[0][0])} {f1(pts[0][1])}"
  for k in 0 ..< 8:
    let (p0, p1, p2, p3) = (pts[(k + 7) mod 8], pts[k], pts[(k + 1) mod 8], pts[(k + 2) mod 8])
    let c1 = (p1[0] + (p2[0] - p0[0]) / 6, p1[1] + (p2[1] - p0[1]) / 6)
    let c2 = (p2[0] - (p3[0] - p1[0]) / 6, p2[1] - (p3[1] - p1[1]) / 6)
    result &= &"C{f1(c1[0])} {f1(c1[1])} {f1(c2[0])} {f1(c2[1])} {f1(p2[0])} {f1(p2[1])}"
  result &= "Z"

proc neckPath(c1: (float, float), r1: float, c2: (float, float), r2: float, hw: float): string =
  ## a pinched neck joining two round bodies, waist half-width hw (a simple version of the garden devlog's)
  let (dx, dy) = (c2[0] - c1[0], c2[1] - c1[1])
  let d = sqrt(dx * dx + dy * dy)
  let (ux, uy) = (dx / d, dy / d)
  let (nx, ny) = (-uy, ux)
  let a = 0.9
  proc p(c: (float, float), r, sx, sy: float): (float, float) = (c[0] + r * (sx * ux + sy * nx), c[1] + r * (sx * uy + sy * ny))
  let p1 = p(c1, r1, cos(a), sin(a))
  let q1 = p(c1, r1, cos(a), -sin(a))
  let p2 = p(c2, r2, -cos(a), sin(a))
  let q2 = p(c2, r2, -cos(a), -sin(a))
  let m = ((c1[0] + c2[0]) / 2, (c1[1] + c2[1]) / 2)
  let w1 = (m[0] + nx * hw, m[1] + ny * hw)
  let w2 = (m[0] - nx * hw, m[1] - ny * hw)
  &"M{f1(c1[0])} {f1(c1[1])}L{f1(p1[0])} {f1(p1[1])}Q{f1(w1[0])} {f1(w1[1])} {f1(p2[0])} {f1(p2[1])}" &
    &"L{f1(c2[0])} {f1(c2[1])}L{f1(q2[0])} {f1(q2[1])}Q{f1(w2[0])} {f1(w2[1])} {f1(q1[0])} {f1(q1[1])}Z"

proc playSvg(st: Style): string =
  var s = &"""<svg class="board" viewBox="0 0 {f1(svgW)} {f1(svgH)}" role="img" aria-label="Parloku board in the {st.name} style">"""
  s &= "<defs>"
  if st.rough:
    s &= &"""<filter id="rough-{st.id}" x="-10%" y="-10%" width="120%" height="120%"><feTurbulence type="fractalNoise" baseFrequency=".09" numOctaves="2" seed="{st.id.len}"/>""" &
      &"""<feDisplacementMap in="SourceGraphic" scale="3.2"/></filter>"""
  for k, (w, path) in words:
    if spelled[k] == 0: continue
    var d = ""
    for i in 0 ..< spelled[k]:
      let (x, y) = cellCentre(path[i][0], path[i][1])
      d &= (if i == 0: "M" else: "L") & f1(x) & " " & f1(y)
    if spelled[k] == 1: d &= "h.01"
    # round joins only round the outer corners; each inner (concave) corner gets a fillet, in white for the
    # outer edge of the band and in black for its inner edge (same construction as the game's overlay)
    proc fillets(w, r: float, colour: string): string =
      for i in 1 ..< spelled[k] - 1:
        let (c, p, n) = (cellCentre(path[i][0], path[i][1]), cellCentre(path[i-1][0], path[i-1][1]), cellCentre(path[i+1][0], path[i+1][1]))
        let a = ((c[0] - p[0]) / cs, (c[1] - p[1]) / cs)
        let b = ((n[0] - c[0]) / cs, (n[1] - c[1]) / cs)
        if abs(a[0] - b[0]) + abs(a[1] - b[1]) < 0.01: continue   # straight on
        let kx = c[0] + w * (b[0] - a[0])
        let ky = c[1] + w * (b[1] - a[1])
        result &= &"""<path d="M{f1(kx)} {f1(ky)}L{f1(kx - a[0] * r)} {f1(ky - a[1] * r)}Q{f1(kx)} {f1(ky)} {f1(kx + b[0] * r)} {f1(ky + b[1] * r)}Z" fill="{colour}"/>"""
    let (outer, band) = (cs * 0.47, 3.0)
    s &= &"""<mask id="wl-{st.id}-{k}" maskUnits="userSpaceOnUse" x="0" y="0" width="{f1(svgW)}" height="{f1(svgH)}">""" &
      &"""<path d="{d}" fill="none" stroke="#fff" stroke-width="{f1(2 * outer)}" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"/>""" &
      fillets(outer, 16, "#fff") &
      &"""<path d="{d}" fill="none" stroke="#000" stroke-width="{f1(2 * (outer - band))}" stroke-linecap="round" stroke-linejoin="round"/>""" &
      fillets(outer - band, 16 + band, "#000") & "</mask>"
  s &= "</defs>"
  # board
  s &= &"""<rect x="{f1(x0 - 4)}" y="{f1(y0 - 4)}" width="{f1(boardW + 8)}" height="{f1(boardW + 8)}" rx="12" fill="{st.card}"/>"""
  for r in 0..5:
    for c in 0..5:
      let fill = if ((r div 2) + (c div 3)) mod 2 == 0: st.ca else: st.cb
      s &= &"""<rect x="{f1(x0 + c.float * cs)}" y="{f1(y0 + r.float * cs)}" width="{f1(cs)}" height="{f1(cs)}" fill="{fill}" stroke="{mix(st.boxline, st.card, 0.82)}" stroke-width="1"/>"""
  for c in [3]:
    s &= &"""<rect x="{f1(x0 + c.float * cs - 1.25)}" y="{f1(y0)}" width="2.5" height="{f1(boardW)}" fill="{st.boxline}" opacity=".85"/>"""
  for r in [2, 4]:
    s &= &"""<rect x="{f1(x0)}" y="{f1(y0 + r.float * cs - 1.25)}" width="{f1(boardW)}" height="2.5" fill="{st.boxline}" opacity=".85"/>"""
  s &= &"""<rect x="{f1(x0)}" y="{f1(y0)}" width="{f1(boardW)}" height="{f1(boardW)}" fill="none" stroke="{st.boxline}" stroke-width="2.5" rx="3"/>"""
  # tiles on the board
  for (r, c) in placedCells():
    let (x, y) = cellCentre(r, c)
    let ch = solution[r][c]
    s &= tileSvg(st, x, y, cs * 0.38, idx(ch), ch)
    if (r, c) in givens:
      s &= &"""<circle cx="{f1(x)}" cy="{f1(y)}" r="{f1(cs * 0.31)}" fill="none" stroke="{st.ink}" stroke-width="1" stroke-dasharray="1.5 2.5" opacity=".45"/>"""
  # word outlines: a ring before the first letter, then the outline of the spelled path
  for k, (w, path) in words:
    if spelled[k] == 0:
      let (x, y) = cellCentre(path[0][0], path[0][1])
      s &= &"""<circle cx="{f1(x)}" cy="{f1(y)}" r="{f1(cs * 0.44)}" fill="none" stroke="{st.w[k]}" stroke-width="3.2"/>"""
    else:
      s &= &"""<rect x="0" y="0" width="{f1(svgW)}" height="{f1(svgH)}" fill="{st.w[k]}" mask="url(#wl-{st.id}-{k})"/>"""
  # six budding letters below the board, one of them mid-division
  let r0 = 21.0
  for i in 0..5:
    let (x, _) = cellCentre(0, i)
    if i == budLetter:
      let mother = (x, rowY)
      let bud = (x + 22, rowY - 31)

      s &= &"""<g{filt}><path d="{neckPath(mother, r0 * 0.96, bud, rb, 6.5)}" fill="{st.g[i]}"/></g>"""

    else:
      s &= tileSvg(st, x, rowY, r0, i, letters[i])
  s & "</svg>"

proc rnd(seed: var uint32): float =
  seed = seed * 1664525'u32 + 1013904223'u32
  float(seed shr 8) / float(1 shl 24)

proc sketch(st: Style, motif: string): string =
  ## a small abstract sketch made only from the style's palette: a reminder of the work, not a copy of it
  var seed = uint32(abs(hash(st.id & motif)) mod 100000 + 7)
  let W = 160.0
  let H = 100.0
  let g = st.g
  let dk = st.w
  var s = &"""<svg viewBox="0 0 {W} {H}" aria-hidden="true"><rect width="{W}" height="{H}" fill="{st.card}"/>"""
  template pick(): string = g[int(rnd(seed) * 6) mod 6]
  let kind = case motif
    of "dots", "pumpkin", "net", "mosaic", "gond", "gond2": "dots"
    of "rings", "spiral", "petals", "target", "swan": "rings"
    of "nested", "interaction", "plain", "constellation": "nested"
    of "grid", "goldgrid", "pencil", "blocks", "room", "boxes", "counting": "grid"
    of "bands", "pale", "pale2", "swash", "totem", "carlton", "rules", "sails": "bands"
    of "waves", "beds", "snake", "fountain": "waves"
    of "blobs", "leaves", "seeds", "flowers", "apple", "birds", "fish", "tree", "offset": "blobs"
    of "bottles", "bottles2", "towers", "cone", "puppets", "hatch": "bottles"
    of "houses", "town", "house", "walls", "pool", "gilardi", "stage", "journey", "stairs", "cars", "boats": "walls"
    of "jigsaw", "slots", "planks", "patch", "cutout", "ship", "plate", "mask": "jigsaw"
    of "chevron", "ndebele", "ndebele2", "altar", "face": "chevron"
    else: "type"
  case kind
  of "dots":
    for k in 0 ..< 26:
      s &= &"""<circle cx="{f1(rnd(seed) * W)}" cy="{f1(rnd(seed) * H)}" r="{f1(3 + rnd(seed) * 9)}" fill="{pick()}"/>"""
    if motif == "pumpkin": s &= &"""<ellipse cx="80" cy="56" rx="38" ry="30" fill="{g[1]}" stroke="{dk[1]}" stroke-width="3"/>"""
  of "rings":
    for k in 0 ..< 6:
      s &= &"""<circle cx="{f1(50 + k.float * 4)}" cy="50" r="{f1(44 - k.float * 7)}" fill="{g[k]}"/>"""
    s &= &"""<circle cx="122" cy="40" r="22" fill="{g[4]}"/><circle cx="122" cy="40" r="12" fill="{g[0]}"/>"""
  of "nested":
    for k in 0 ..< 4:
      let sz = 90 - k.float * 20
      s &= &"""<rect x="{f1(80 - sz / 2)}" y="{f1(96 - sz - k.float * 2)}" width="{f1(sz)}" height="{f1(sz)}" fill="{g[(k * 2 + motif.len) mod 6]}"/>"""
  of "grid":
    for r in 0..3:
      for c in 0..5:
        let fill = if rnd(seed) < 0.55: pick() else: st.cb
        s &= &"""<rect x="{f1(12 + c.float * 23)}" y="{f1(8 + r.float * 22)}" width="21" height="20" rx="2" fill="{fill}"/>"""
    if motif in ["goldgrid", "pencil"]:
      for k in 0..10: s &= &"""<line x1="0" y1="{f1(k.float * 10)}" x2="{W}" y2="{f1(k.float * 10)}" stroke="{st.muted}" stroke-width=".5" opacity=".6"/>"""
  of "bands":
    for k in 0 ..< 8:
      s &= &"""<rect x="0" y="{f1(k.float * H / 8)}" width="{W}" height="{f1(H / 8 + 0.5)}" fill="{g[k mod 6]}"/>"""
  of "waves":
    for k in 0 ..< 6:
      var d = &"M0 {f1(12 + k.float * 16)}"
      for x in countup(0, 160, 20):
        d &= &" Q{f1(x.float + 10)} {f1(k.float * 16 + (if (x div 20) mod 2 == 0: 0.0 else: 24.0))} {f1(x.float + 20)} {f1(12 + k.float * 16)}"
      s &= &"""<path d="{d}" fill="none" stroke="{(if k mod 2 == 0: dk[0] else: g[k])}" stroke-width="6" stroke-linecap="round"/>"""
  of "blobs":
    for k in 0 ..< 7:
      s &= &"""<path d="{blobPath(rnd(seed) * W, rnd(seed) * H, 12 + rnd(seed) * 16, k + motif.len, 0.22)}" fill="{pick()}"/>"""
  of "bottles":
    var x = 14.0
    for k in 0 ..< 6:
      let w = 16 + rnd(seed) * 10
      let h = 40 + rnd(seed) * 40
      s &= &"""<rect x="{f1(x)}" y="{f1(92 - h)}" width="{f1(w)}" height="{f1(h)}" rx="{f1(w / 3)}" fill="{g[k]}"/>"""
      s &= &"""<rect x="{f1(x + w / 2 - 3)}" y="{f1(84 - h)}" width="6" height="12" rx="2" fill="{g[k]}"/>"""
      x += w + 6
    s &= &"""<rect x="0" y="92" width="{W}" height="8" fill="{st.cb}"/>"""
  of "walls":
    s &= &"""<rect width="{W}" height="{H}" fill="{g[motif.len mod 6]}"/>"""
    s &= &"""<rect x="18" y="20" width="58" height="80" fill="{g[(motif.len + 2) mod 6]}"/>"""
    s &= &"""<rect x="76" y="44" width="70" height="56" fill="{g[(motif.len + 4) mod 6]}"/>"""
    s &= &"""<rect x="90" y="58" width="16" height="22" fill="{st.card}"/><rect x="40" y="38" width="14" height="18" fill="{st.card}"/>"""
  of "jigsaw":
    for r in 0..2:
      for c in 0..3:
        s &= &"""<rect x="{f1(8 + c.float * 37)}" y="{f1(6 + r.float * 30)}" width="35" height="28" rx="3" fill="{pick()}" stroke="{st.ink}" stroke-width="1.2"/>"""
        s &= &"""<circle cx="{f1(43 + c.float * 37)}" cy="{f1(20 + r.float * 30)}" r="5" fill="{pick()}" stroke="{st.ink}" stroke-width="1.2"/>"""
  of "chevron":
    for k in 0 ..< 5:
      let x = 10 + k.float * 29
      s &= &"""<path d="M{f1(x)} 86L{f1(x + 14)} 20L{f1(x + 28)} 86Z" fill="{g[k]}" stroke="#151515" stroke-width="4"/>"""
    s &= &"""<rect x="0" y="86" width="{W}" height="14" fill="#151515"/>"""
  else:
    let word = st.name.split(' ')[0].toUpperAscii[0..min(3, st.name.split(' ')[0].len - 1)]
    for k, ch in word:
      s &= &"""<rect x="{f1(10 + k.float * 36)}" y="{f1(22 + (k mod 2).float * 10)}" width="34" height="46" rx="8" fill="{g[k mod 6]}"/>"""
      s &= &"""<text x="{f1(27 + k.float * 36)}" y="{f1(46 + (k mod 2).float * 10)}" class="glyph" font-size="30" fill="{st.ink}">{ch}</text>"""
  s & "</svg>"
