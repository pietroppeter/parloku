DEVLOG · 01

Ten skins for a letter game

Pietro asked for a Parloku that is less cluttered, in pastel colours, that his seven-year-old daughter would like and grown-ups would still find beautiful. Instead of guessing one answer, here are ten, each after an artist or a tradition. Pick one below and play; the notes under the board change with it.

01 · WHY THESE TENHow I picked the artists

I started from the request rather than from a list of favourite artists. Three filters:

  1. They made things for children, or children love what they made. Munari, Tullet, Lionni, Bruna and Taro Gomi are picture-book makers first. Miró, Matisse, Delaunay and Maija Isola are not, but their work is what children draw when you hand them scissors and paint: dots, stars, blobs, bold flowers.
  2. Their style is made of a few rules, not of pictures. A Parloku skin only has six tile colours, six letter colours, one tile shape, one kind of line, one texture and three typefaces. An artist whose look survives being reduced to that (Munari's square and circle, Bruna's black outline, Delaunay's rings, Lionni's torn paper) translates; one whose look lives in drawing characters or scenes does not. That is also the line I didn't cross: no characters, no copied motifs, no rabbit. Only palettes, shapes, edges and type, borrowed as principles.
  3. A spread of places, as Pietro asked: Italy, France, Spain, the Netherlands, Finland and Japan.

One of the ten isn't a person. Wagashi are Japanese seasonal sweets; their palette (sakura pink, matcha, yuzu, chestnut) is one of the gentlest I know, and I wanted one skin that is calm rather than loud. The old maiolica look stays as an eleventh option, for comparison.

02 · ANATOMYWhat a skin is made of

Before, colours were written into the Nim code that draws the board. Now the Nim view only names things: a tile is a group with class g3 holding three shapes (sq, rd, ci); a cell is cell ca; a word path is wp c1. A skin is a block of CSS that answers every name. These are all the elements a skin decides:

  • Ground and ink: the page background, the card behind the board, the text colour and a muted text colour.
  • Six tile colours and six letter colours (--g0…--g5, --i0…--i5), one pair per letter.
  • Five clue inks (--c0…--c4) for the numbered markers, the word paths and the chips above the board.
  • Tile shape: square, rounded, round, or alternating by letter. All three shapes are drawn and CSS shows one, so changing shape costs nothing at runtime.
  • Line: the tile outline (none, hairline, or a thick black contour) and the board's box lines.
  • Texture: an SVG filter that roughens edges, either finely (pencil, torn paper) or coarsely (scissors).
  • Board: frame, the two alternating cell tints, the clash and tutorial highlights.
  • Type: heading, body and letter typefaces, and the weight of the letters.
  • Furniture: card radius, borders, shadows. Most skins drop shadows altogether.

03 · PRINCIPLESHow I judge if a skin works

"Less cluttered" and "more playful" are taste, but most of what makes them work can be checked. I used five principles:

  1. Every letter must be easy to read on its tile. Letter against tile contrast of at least 4.5:1, the WCAG level for normal text, even though the letters are big and bold (where 3:1 would already pass).
  2. Every tile must be told apart from the others and from the board. Colour distance ΔE of at least 15 between any two tile colours, and between a tile and the cell it sits on, unless the skin draws an outline.
  3. Information keeps its colour; decoration loses it. Clue numbers, word paths and muted text get at least 4.5:1, so the things you need while playing never fade. The decoration that carried no information went: the patterned band, the hard shadows and the row/column labels (now shown only during the tutorial, where the hints name cells).
  4. One idea per skin. One shape rule, one line rule, at most one texture. If a skin needs two tricks, it is cluttered.
  5. Playful means soft edges and a light ground. Round or hand-made shapes, warm or white backgrounds, friendly type. The test is squinting at a phone screenshot: you should see six soft colours and the letters, nothing else.

The first two and the third are numbers, so the page measures them while it builds, straight from the CSS you are looking at:

proc luminance(c: Rgb): float = 0.2126 * lin(c.r) + 0.7152 * lin(c.g) + 0.0722 * lin(c.b)
proc contrast(a, b: string): float =
  let (x, y) = (luminance(hex(a)), luminance(hex(b)))
  (max(x, y) + 0.05) / (min(x, y) + 0.05)
proc lab(s: string): (float, float, float) =
  let c = hex(s)
  let (r, g, b) = (lin(c.r), lin(c.g), lin(c.b))
  let x = (r * 0.4124 + g * 0.3576 + b * 0.1805) / 0.95047
  let y = r * 0.2126 + g * 0.7152 + b * 0.0722
  let z = (r * 0.0193 + g * 0.1192 + b * 0.9505) / 1.08883

04 · WHAT THE AUDIT CAUGHTFixes made while writing this

Writing the principles down and measuring them turned up real problems in the first version of the skins, all fixed in this one:

  • White letters on pastel tiles. Tullet's blue, Miró's blue, Matisse's blue and one maiolica glaze had white letters at 2.1 to 3.3:1. Pastel and white text never work together; those letters are now dark, and Matisse's blue is a deeper cobalt so its white letter reaches 5.8:1.
  • Yellow and orange clue inks. Every skin had a sunny clue colour at about 2:1 on white, which made a word path and its white number hard to see. All clue inks are now darker versions of the same hue, at 4.6:1 or more.
  • Muted text (stats, empty clue letters, "show machinery") was 2.7 to 3.6:1. It is now at least 4.6:1.
  • Two wagashi tiles were twins (sakura and azuki, ΔE 13). Azuki became chestnut.
  • Motion. The pulsing tutorial target and the confetti now stop when the device asks for reduced motion, and buttons get a visible keyboard focus ring.

What the audit can't fix, and is still open:

  • Small cells on phones. On a 390 px screen a cell is about 35 px, under the 44 px usually advised for touch. Snapping makes drops forgiving, but a phone layout with less margin around the board would help.
  • The board isn't keyboard-playable yet. Tiles can only be dragged; a tap-a-stack, tap-a-cell mode would serve both keyboards and small fingers.
  • Textured skins cost more. Lionni, Gomi and Matisse run an SVG filter on every tile; fine on a recent phone, worth checking on an old one.
  • Fonts come from Google Fonts. Offline, each skin falls back to a system face, which flattens the hand-made ones.

05 · NEXTChoosing

The real test of "something a seven-year-old might like" is a seven-year-old. Play a few boards in each skin and keep the two or three that survive. The winners become the default, and the rest go back in the drawer.
